Capstone Project — Aquarium Monitoring & Treatment

Capstone Project · 2024

Aquarium Monitoring and Treatment System Based on the Internet of Things (IoT)

An IoT system that watches aquarium water quality in real time and treats it on its own — reading water temperature and turbidity, reporting to the Blynk app, and renewing the water automatically when it turns dirty.

How it works

  • Water temperature drops to 27 °C or below — the LED indicator lights up
  • Turbidity reaches 250 NTU — the drain pump empties the dirty water, then the refill pump draws clean water back in
  • Readings alternate on a 16×2 LCD, while the Blynk dashboard shows the same values and their history

Team

Built with Agnes Elandriyana Ekasiwi and M. Dahlan, supervised by Dodi Hariadi, S.T., M.Eng. — Computer Engineering, Universitas Teknologi Yogyakarta.

Tools & materials

  • ESP32 microcontroller
  • DS18B20 water temperature sensor and turbidity sensor
  • 4-channel relay module and two water pumps — one for draining, one for refilling
  • 16×2 I²C LCD, LED indicator, jumper wires
  • Double-layer PCB, hose and 5 V adapter

Software

  • Arduino IDE (C/C++) and the Blynk IoT platform

Result

Across five test runs the system tracked 26.7–28.7 °C and 13–506 NTU. Every time turbidity crossed 250 NTU the drain and refill pumps renewed the water; at 17 NTU they stayed off.

Internship — IoT-Based Bridge SHMS

Internship · 2025

Structural Health Monitoring System for Bridges Based on the Internet of Things (IoT)

A Structural Health Monitoring System that reads a bridge’s vibration and load in real time and reports its condition through a Node-RED dashboard — built during an internship at the Public Works and Spatial Planning Office of Kebumen Regency.

How it works

  • An MPU6050 accelerometer reads vibration, acceleration and tilt; a load cell reads weight, force, stress, strain, change in length and deflection
  • An ESP32 publishes the readings over MQTT to a Node-RED dashboard and a MySQL database, while a 16×2 LCD shows them on site
  • Recorded data is exported to Microsoft Excel and processed in SeismoSignal with FFT to obtain the bridge’s actual frequency and deflection

Site

Pelangi Luk Ulo Bridge — a 60 m Warren truss bridge over the Luk Ulo river at Pejagoan, Kebumen, built in 1997. The sensors were mounted at mid-span on the lower girder.

Tools & materials

  • ESP32 microcontroller (Wi-Fi, 48 GPIO)
  • MPU6050 accelerometer — 6-DOF IMU, 16-bit, I²C
  • Load cell with HX711 amplifier module — 24-bit ADC
  • 16×2 I²C LCD, breadboard, jumper wires, micro USB cable
  • Double-type clamp to lock the sensor onto the bridge girder

Software

  • Arduino IDE (C/C++), MQTT, Node-RED, MySQL, Microsoft Excel and SeismoSignal

Result

Peak acceleration at mid-span reached 0.09 g (x), 0.09 g (y) and 0.10 g (z). A measured frequency of 2.19727 Hz against a natural frequency of 2.1946 Hz from SAP2000 gave a frequency ratio of 0.121% — level 1, meaning no immediate repair, only routine maintenance. Actual strain of 0.0003% stayed under the 0.00112% limit, and actual deflection of 0.0698 m stayed under the 0.075 m limit.

Final Project — Aquarium Monitoring & Control System

Final Project · 2026

Design and Development of a Water Quality Monitoring and Control System with Automatic Fish Feeding for Aquariums Based on a Mobile Application

An automatic fish feeding device with IoT-based water quality monitoring, integrated with an Android app for monitoring and both automatic and manual control.

How it works

  • A DS18B20 probe reads water temperature and a turbidity sensor reads water clarity; an ESP32 sends both to Firebase Realtime Database, where an Android app shows them live
  • Temperature is held between 25 and 27 °C — below 25 °C the heater switches on, above 27 °C it stops and a pump starts
  • Above 10 NTU the system changes the water: one pump drains for two minutes, then a second refills for two minutes
  • Feeding runs three times a day — 08:00, 13:00 and 17:00 — with the servo turning 90° for five seconds, and can also be triggered by hand from the app

App

Three screens built in Android Studio: Home, Monitoring Charts and Feeding Schedule — with an AUTO or MANUAL mode and manual buttons for the pumps, the heater and feeding.

Tools & materials

  • ESP32 microcontroller (Wi-Fi, 48 GPIO)
  • DS18B20 water temperature sensor (±0.5%, 1-Wire) and a turbidity sensor (optical, 0–1000 NTU)
  • Two 12 V DC submersible pumps (±800–1500 L/h) for draining and refilling
  • Aquarium heater (25 W), servo motor (0–180°, PWM) for the feeder, relay module and 5 V adapter
  • Mechanical sponge filter (250 L/h) and a 40 × 25 × 25 cm aquarium

Wiring

DS18B20 → GPIO 14 · turbidity → GPIO 34 · heater relay → GPIO 4 · pump relays → GPIO 18 and GPIO 5 · servo → GPIO 26.

Software

  • Arduino IDE (C/C++), Firebase Realtime Database and Android Studio

Result

The DS18B20 held an average error of 3.32% — 96.68% accuracy. The turbidity sensor separated clear, cloudy and dirty water and ran the pumps automatically on the cloudy and dirty readings. The servo fed on all three daily schedules at a 90° turn for five seconds, temperature and turbidity appeared in the app in real time, every manual command was executed, and history logged every minute rendered as charts.

Internet of Things

“IoT Innovation to Improve Community Welfare”

FusionTech Challenge · 2025

IoT-Based CO Monitoring and Control System

An IoT system that reads carbon monoxide (CO) levels in the air and brings them back down on its own. An MQ-135 gas sensor samples the air while a Wemos D1 R32 (ESP32) board publishes the readings over MQTT to a Node-RED dashboard and a MySQL database; a 16×2 LCD shows the live value on the device. Past 100 ppm a blower fan starts, and past 200 ppm a water pump feeds a mist sprinkler that scrubs the gas out of the air. In testing, running the fan and sprinkler together pulled CO down from 1,463 ppm to 79 ppm.

Tools & materials

  • Wemos D1 R32 (ESP32) board and MQ-135 gas sensor
  • 4-channel relay module and 16×2 I²C LCD
  • 12 V DC water pump, mist sprinkler and CPU fan blower
  • One-layer PCB, acrylic housing, 12 V 2 A power adapter

Software

  • Arduino IDE (C/C++), MQTT broker, Node-RED dashboard and MySQL
